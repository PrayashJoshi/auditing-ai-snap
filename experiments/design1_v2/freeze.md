# Design 1 v2 freeze: confirmatory reader experiment

This file fixes the design, inputs, prompt, scoring rules and hypotheses of the second reader experiment. It is to be committed before the first model call; results are then cited as "pre-specified, commit `<hash of the commit that adds this file>`". Nothing below is reworded after that commit. Everything in this file was produced with zero model calls.

The testbed is **QC-grounded synthetic case files**: households, amounts and error structure come from the FY2024 SNAP QC public-use file; every document a reader sees is generated by our code (`snapqc.packets.build_case` plus the deposit records in `snapqc.evidence_states`). All results are results **in this testbed**.

Feeds RQ2 (how accurate an AI reader is) and, through the deterministic comparators, RQ1 (where existing review rules fall short). Numbering used in this file: RQ1 real QC data and existing rules, RQ2 reader, RQ3 filer.

The stray-credit class is 48 households, each shown under both labels: 48 x 2 = 96 inputs. That is the only reading under which the stated sizes reach 264 (96 + 24 + 48 + 96), and it matches the within-household contrast the omission class already uses for its two deposit labels. Every household appears in exactly one class.

Readers: `deepseek-v4-flash`, `qwen3.5-397b`, `gemma-4-31b-it`. Two repeats per input. 264 x 3 x 2 = **1,584 calls**. Temperature 0, `max_output_tokens` 8192, concurrency 8, forced tool call (`report_flags` with confidence).

### 1a. Class definitions

- **Income omission:** an earned or unearned group filed at $2 or less with a verified amount above $0. On the 616 households of the human arm (`results/01-data-checks/3d-human-arm.csv`) this gives 185 (122 earned, 63 unearned); the build asserts 185. The application reports the target group as $0.
- **Truthful documented:** the target group has a verified amount above $0 and is reported at that amount, with all its documents in the file.
- **True zero with rent:** the target group is $0 in QC and the household's verified rent is above $0.
- **True zero with one stray credit:** the target group is $0 in QC. The file holds one non-recurring credit, the generator's own own-account savings transfer (amount $40.90 to $219.38, median $138.79 in these 48 files).

### 1b. What is the same in every input

- Shelter and utilities are reported at their verified amounts, and their documents (lease, rent payments, utility bills) are in the file whenever the verified amount is above $0. The other income group is reported at its verified amount with its documents.
- The application shows whole dollars for all four groups; "not provided" never appears.
- The state's utility allowance line.
- The reader instructions and tool of the first reader run, unchanged (same hashes as there).

### 1c. Evidence states

- **none:** no record of the target group.
- **deposits, clear label:** only the target group's bank credits are kept (pay stubs, award letters, notices and income records removed). Credits keep the generator's description (for example `PAYROLL DEP <EMPLOYER>`, `SOC SEC BENEFIT SSA`, `STATE CHILD SUPPORT DISBURSEMENT UNIT`). Income types the generator leaves with no bank trace (self-employment, odd jobs, room rental, contributions, deemed, other and unknown income) get two monthly credits of the verified amount on the 7th, labelled by `UNLINKED_LABEL` in `snapqc/evidence_states.py`.
- **deposits, ambiguous label:** the same credits, same dates and amounts, every description replaced by `ACH CREDIT ELECTRONIC DEPOSIT`.
- **lead:** the none file plus one line: the first reader run's cross-match lead text for the target group (wage records for earned, benefit records for unearned).
- **documented:** the complete file.
- **stray credit, clear label:** the complete file of a true-zero household (it contains the savings transfer as generated).
- **stray credit, ambiguous label:** the same file with the savings transfer's description replaced by `ACH CREDIT ELECTRONIC DEPOSIT`, the same label the ambiguous deposits use. The two files of a household differ in that one line (asserted).

## 6. Dry-run rule

Before the main run, each reader reads 24 inputs once (the first 3 inputs of each of the 8 class and state cells, in file order): 72 calls, logged to `dryrun.jsonl` and not scored. Valid reader output means the call completed with exactly one `report_flags` call whose arguments pass the tool schema, and every flag names one of the four groups at most once (`run_design1_v2.reader_output_valid`). A reader with under 90% valid output (under 22 of 24) is dropped from the main run and reported as dropped (`dryrun-validity.csv`). The main run calls only retained readers.

## 7. Scoring rules (`score_design1_v2.py`)

- The household is the unit. Repeat 1 is primary. Agreement between repeats 1 and 2 is reported beside it, per reader, class and state: on the target flag, and on the whole flag set.
- Primary metrics are on the target group: **hit rate** = share of omission households whose target group is flagged; **false-flag rate** = share of true-zero (or truthful documented) households whose target group is flagged. Flags on either income group and on any group are secondary.
- Wilson 95% intervals for every rate. Invalid outputs are left out of the denominator and counted beside each rate.
- Deterministic comparators are scored on the same 264 inputs: the frozen checker (`checker.check`) and the checker plus the budget-gap rule of the first reader run (re-implemented in `comparators_v2.py`, checked equal to the budget-gap part of `run_design1.run_checker_plus_2_rules` on every input; the first run's second rule, unmatched lead or deposit, is not used). Their results were computed before any call (`comparators.csv`).
- Not computed: precision at balanced prevalence, and any caseload precision. The mix of file types in a real caseload is unknown. Reported instead (`reference-false-flag-rates.csv`): the false-flag rate a reader would need for 80% precision at 80% recall is 0.09% at 0.46% prevalence (known rebuilt omissions among $0 filings) and 0.51% at 2.47% (upper all-cause bracket); with 48 negatives and zero flags the Wilson upper bound is 7.4%. The experiment can refute that a reader reaches such a rate; it cannot confirm it.

## 8. Reader hypotheses

These are the only pre-specified hypotheses. Each is evaluated on repeat 1, target group, for every reader retained after the dry run.

- **Reader hypothesis 1:** at "none", hit rate on omissions minus false-flag rate on true zeros with rent has a 95% interval that includes 0, for each reader. Interval: Newcombe's hybrid score interval for a difference of two independent proportions, built from the two Wilson intervals (24 omission households, 48 true zeros with rent).
- **Reader hypothesis 2:** at "lead", hit rate is at least 0.90 (positive control). Point estimate per reader (at least 22 of 24 when all outputs are valid).
- **Reader hypothesis 3:** with ambiguous labels, at least 2 of 3 readers false-flag at least 20% of stray-credit true zeros. A false flag here is a flag on either income group (earned or unearned): every income group of a stray-credit household is filed at its verified value with its documents (section 1b), so a flag on either is false, and the stray credit is the same line whichever group is the household's target. Point estimate per reader on the 48 stray-credit households under the ambiguous label; the hypothesis holds when at least 2 readers meet it. The target-group rate on the same households is reported beside it. This departs from the target-group rule of section 7 and makes the hypothesis easier to pass than a target-only reading would; it was set before any reader call and before this file was committed.

A failed hypothesis is reported as failed. None is reworded after the freeze. All are statements about readers in this testbed (QC-grounded synthetic case files).

## 10. Open questions

- The stray credit is the generator's own savings transfer ($41 to $219). An income-sized stray credit would be a harder negative; this design keeps the generator's amount.
- The earned lead text names state wage records also for the one self-employment omission household (row 13015 has self-employment; wage records would not normally show it).
- Reader instructions are reused unchanged from the first reader run, so they still define "not provided", which no input here contains.
- Reading of reader hypothesis 1: with no false flags, 3 of 24 hits at "none" is enough to fail it, and the budget-gap comparator (11 of 24 against 8 of 48) fails it too. A reader that fails hypothesis 1 therefore shows that it reads the budget gap, which is evidence the file holds, not that it reads evidence about the missing income. This reading is fixed here, before any call.
- Hypothesis 3 when a reader is dropped in the dry run: the threshold stays at 2 readers, counted among the retained readers.
